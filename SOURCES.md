# Sources & asset manifest

## Historical references used for the script

The narration distinguishes established facts from debated interpretations ("माना जाता है", "शायद", "विद्वानों में बहस").
Reconstructions are labelled on screen as **पुनर्निर्माण चित्रण** or **सांकेतिक चित्रण**.

| Claim in video | Reference |
|---|---|
| Mature Harappan phase c. 2600–1900 BCE; the civilisation was spread over present-day Pakistan, NW India and Afghanistan | J. M. Kenoyer, *Ancient Cities of the Indus Valley Civilization* (1998); G. Possehl, *The Indus Civilization: A Contemporary Perspective* (2002) |
| Mohenjo-daro: planned grid, baked bricks, covered drains, wells, the Great Bath (~12 × 7 m, ~2.4 m deep) | UNESCO World Heritage listing "Archaeological Ruins at Moenjodaro" (whc.unesco.org/en/list/138); Kenoyer 1998 |
| Standardised brick proportions of 4:2:1, and standardised cubical chert weights | Kenoyer 1998; Possehl 2002 |
| Rakhigarhi, Dholavira, Lothal, Kalibangan, Harappa | Archaeological Survey of India; UNESCO listing "Dholavira: a Harappan City" (2021) |
| Trade with Mesopotamia; "Meluhha" is widely identified with the Indus region | Possehl 2002; R. Wright, *The Ancient Indus* (2010) |
| Gradual de-urbanisation c. 1900–1300 BCE; a shift to smaller settlements to the east | Wright 2010; Possehl 2002 |
| Weakening of the monsoon around the 4.2 ka event | Giosan et al., "Fluvial landscapes of the Harappan civilization", *PNAS* 109 (2012); Dixit et al., *Geology* (2014) |
| Ghaggar-Hakra drying, with timing debated | Giosan et al. 2012; Singh et al., *Nature Communications* 8 (2017) |
| Wheeler's "Aryan invasion / massacre" interpretation (1940s–50s), and its rejection | G. F. Dales, "The Mythical Massacre at Mohenjo-daro", *Expedition* 6(3) (1964) |
| Rakhigarhi ancient DNA, which shows no Steppe ancestry in the sampled individual | Shinde et al., *Cell* 179 (2019) |
| The Indus script is still undeciphered | Possehl, *Indus Age: The Writing System* (1996); Wright 2010 |
